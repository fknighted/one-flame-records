## Short answer

Send a message through the contact page on the website, choosing "Artist submission" as the reason, and include a short bio, a description of your sound, and links to your best work. No attachments are needed at that stage. The label says every submission is listened to by the team and that they will get back to you within two weeks. If the music fits, the next step is a call — not paperwork.

## Details

The site sets out three steps under the heading "Simple. Three steps.":

1. **Get in touch.** Send a message through the contact page with a brief biography, your sound, and links to your best work. No attachments at this stage.
2. **We review your music.** Every submission is listened to by the label team. They look for originality, craft and cultural rootedness. They will get back to you within two weeks.
3. **Conversation, not a contract.** If the music fits, they set up a call — "no pressure, no paperwork yet."

**Genres the label works with**, as listed on the site: roots reggae, dancehall, conscious music, rocksteady, ska, and Afrobeats or Afrodancehall. The page adds: "If your sound doesn't fit a neat category but feels connected to Caribbean music culture, reach out anyway."

**What the label offers.** One Flame signs artists at every stage, "from emerging bedroom producers to established performers", and works with them on production, releases, visual identity and distribution. Its stated deal principle is "You keep your publishing. We handle the platform."

**Invitation-only application forms.** The label also issues private application links, usually reached by scanning a QR code it hands out. Those open a fuller form asking for stage name, legal name, email, phone, genres, socials and a message. They are not public and they expire — an expired one shows "Link expired".

## Common questions

**Do you accept demos by email attachment?** The site asks for links rather than attachments. "No attachments needed to start a conversation."

**Will I definitely get a reply?** The site says the team gets back within two weeks, and the Terms add that submitting material "does not guarantee a response or signing offer." Treat two weeks as the label's stated aim.

**Do I have to pay to submit?** No. Nothing on the site charges for a submission.

## Escalate

Escalate anything about deal terms, royalty splits, advances, ownership, or an application's individual status. Only the label team can answer those.

## Do not say

- Do not describe contract terms, royalty percentages, advances or contract length. None are published.
- Do not promise a signing, a reply, or a review outcome.
- Do not hand out or guess an application link or code. Those are issued by the label.
